**Date:** June 2026  
**Focus:** Jailbreak template effectiveness against safety-trained models

## Core Observation

Jailbreak success is highly template-dependent and model-dependent. The gap between "safety-trained" and "actually robust" is large — a single effective template can collapse refusal rates from >90% to <5%.

## Key Findings

1. **Roleplay templates are disproportionately effective.** A 2023 roleplay template (single user turn, no system-message access) dropped StrongREJECT jailbreak rate from 0.6% to **77.8%** on DSv4 Pro. This suggests safety training is brittle to framing shifts, not adversarial optimization.

2. **Developer mode and DAN persist.** Despite years of patching, persona-injection attacks ("Do Anything Now", "Developer Mode enabled") remain effective. Models appear to treat persona switches as context changes that override safety instructions.

3. **Encoding bypasses are model-dependent.** Base64 and unicode tag obfuscation work on some backends but not others. The effectiveness depends on whether the model decodes before safety classification or classifies the encoded string directly.

4. **Hypothetical framing is the stealthiest.** "In a hypothetical scenario where..." and creative-writing framing produce lower detection rates than direct attacks because they do not trigger obvious jailbreak keywords.

## Implication

Safety evaluation must test against a **diverse template library**, not just a single jailbreak method. A model that resists GCG may collapse completely under roleplay framing. Current benchmarks (HarmBench, StrongREJECT) capture only a subset of the attack surface.
