# GuardReasoner Research Brief

> **Note on naming:** The target system was requested as "Guardreadoner" — no such AI safety benchmark or tool exists in the literature. This brief covers **GuardReasoner**, which is almost certainly the intended subject. GuardReasoner is a widely-cited family of reasoning-based LLM/VLM safeguard models from NUS and collaborators. The name "Guardreadoner" appears to be a phonetic or typographic corruption of "GuardReasoner."

---

## 1. What GuardReasoner Is

### Purpose
GuardReasoner is a **reasoning-based safety guardrail model** for Large Language Models (LLMs) and, in later variants, for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and omni-modal systems (text, image, video, audio). Unlike traditional guardrails that output binary safe/unsafe labels, GuardReasoner is trained to produce explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces before rendering a moderation verdict. The core thesis is that **safety is a reasoning problem, not merely a classification problem** — reflexive pattern-matching fails against novel jailbreaks, while deliberate reasoning generalizes better.

### Origin & Authors
- **Primary paper:** "GuardReasoner: Towards Reasoning-based LLM Safeguards" (arXiv:2501.18492, Jan 2025)
- **Core authors:** Yue Liu, Hongcheng Gao, Shengfang Zhai, Yufei He, Jun Xia, Zhengyu Hu, Yulin Chen, Xihong Yang, Jiaheng Zhang, Stan Z. Li, Hui Xiong, **Bryan Hooi** (National University of Singapore)
- **Affiliations:** National University of Singapore (NUS), with collaborators across China and the US
- **GitHub:** https://github.com/yueliu1999/GuardReasoner
- **HuggingFace:** Models, datasets, and code publicly released

### Family of Models
GuardReasoner has evolved into a **model family** with four major variants:

| Variant | Paper | Date | Modality | Key Innovation |
|---------|-------|------|----------|----------------|
| **GuardReasoner** (text) | arXiv:2501.18492 | Jan 2025 | Text | Reasoning SFT + Hard Sample DPO for text guardrails |
| **GuardReasoner-VL** | arXiv:2505.11049 | May 2025 | Text + Image | Reinforced reasoning for VLM safety (NeurIPS 2025) |
| **GuardReasoner-Omni** | arXiv:2602.03328 | Feb 2026 | Text, Image, Video, Audio | First unified omni-modal reasoning guardrail |
| **GuardReasoner-Omni (v2)** | arXiv:2602.03328v2 | May 2026 | Text, Image, Video, Audio | RL with concise correctness reward for efficiency |

### Current Status
- **Active development:** The most recent variant (GuardReasoner-Omni) was updated in May 2026
- **Open-source:** Code, training data (GuardReasonerTrain), and model weights (1B, 3B, 8B/7B scales) are publicly available
- **Cited widely:** Referenced in SoK papers on jailbreak guardrails, multimodal safety surveys, and benchmark papers
- **Deployed models:** Available on HuggingFace for direct inference

## 2. Methodology

### Core Training Paradigm: Two-Stage
GuardReasoner is trained via a **two-stage pipeline** designed to first unlock reasoning capability, then refine it on hard cases:

#### Stage 1: R-SFT (Reasoning Supervised Fine-Tuning)
- **Objective:** Teach the guard model to "think out loud" before judging
- **Data:** GuardReasonerTrain dataset — 127K text samples with **460K detailed reasoning steps** (for the text variant); 181K samples for the omni variant
- **Synthesis:** Reasoning traces are generated by strong teacher models (GPT-4o, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, Mimo-v2.5 for audio) and refined via LLM-as-Judge auditing
- **Output format:** The model produces structured reasoning followed by a safety verdict

#### Stage 2: HS-DPO (Hard Sample Direct Preference Optimization)
- **Objective:** Strengthen reasoning on adversarial and edge cases
- **Method:** Mine hard negative samples (k=8 sampling) and use DPO to prefer correct reasoning chains over incorrect ones
- **KL constraint:** β = 0.01 to prevent divergence
- **Stabilization:** Mix R-SFT loss during HS-DPO to prevent model collapse (trade-off weight = 2)

### Threat Model
GuardReasoner assumes an **adaptive adversary** that can:
- Craft novel jailbreaks not seen in training (OOD generalization is a key goal)
- Use multi-turn conversations to gradually elicit harmful outputs
- Operate across modalities (for VL/Omni variants)
- Exploit surface-level pattern matching in traditional classifiers

The core insight is that traditional guardrails are **sophisticated pattern matchers** that fail when attackers change surface features while preserving underlying harmful intent. GuardReasoner addresses this by requiring the model to reason about *why* content is harmful.

### Capabilities Tested
GuardReasoner evaluates on **three guardrail tasks**:

1. **Prompt Harmfulness Detection** — Is the user input harmful?
2. **Response Harmfulness Detection** — Is the model output harmful/refused?
3. **Refusal Detection** — Did the model correctly refuse a harmful request?

These span **13+ benchmarks** across text, with additional benchmarks for image, video, and audio in later variants.

### Model Architecture
- **Base models:** Llama 3.2 (1B, 3B) for text; Qwen2.5-VL (3B, 7B) for VL; custom omni backbones
- **Training:** Full-parameter fine-tuning, BFloat16, DeepSpeed Stage 3, AdamW optimizer
- **Context length:** 2048 tokens
- **Batch size:** 256 (64 accumulate × 1 per device × 4 GPUs)
- **Learning rate:** 5e-6 with cosine scheduler
- **Epochs:** 2.0

## 3. Dataset / Test Cases

### Training Data

#### GuardReasonerTrain (Text)
- **Size:** 127K samples with 460K reasoning steps
- **Composition:** Curated from existing safety datasets + synthetic reasoning annotations
- **Sources:** Aegis, BeaverTails, HarmBench, OpenAI Moderation, SafeRLHF, ToxicChat, WildGuard, XSTest
- **Annotation:** Teacher-distillation from GPT-4o + self-refinement via LLM-as-Judge (3-round auditing)

#### GuardReasoner-OmniTrain (Omni-Modal)
- **Size:** 181K samples across four modalities
- **Text:** Aegis2.0, BeaverTails, ToxicChat, WildGuard
- **Image:** VLGuard, LLaVAGuard, SPA-VL, VLSBench, UnsafeBench
- **Video:** UCF-Crime, XD-Violence, SafeWatch-Bench, VHD, Video-SafetyBench, SafeSora
- **Audio:** MuTox, SACRED-Bench, Jailbreak-AudioBench
- **Annotation:** Qwen3.5-397B-A17B for text/image/video; Mimo-v2.5 for audio; 3-round LLM-as-Judge refinement

### Evaluation Benchmarks

GuardReasoner is evaluated on a comprehensive suite of safety benchmarks:

| Benchmark | Task Type | Description |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **HarmBench** | Prompt/Response | Standard harmful behavior benchmark |
| **ToxicChat** | Prompt | Real-world user-AI conversation toxicity |

## 4. Scoring / Metrics

### Primary Metric: F1 Score
GuardReasoner reports **F1 score (%)** as the primary metric, balancing precision and recall. For safety-critical applications, recall is especially important (missing harmful content is worse than false positives).

### Key Results (GuardReasoner 8B, Text)

| Benchmark | GuardReasoner 8B | LLaMA Guard 3 8B | GPT-4o+CoT | WildGuard 7B |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|------------|--------------|
| HarmBench | 91.86 | 98.94 | 81.98 | 98.90 |
| ToxicChat | 78.79 | 53.12 | 73.43 | 70.80 |
| AegisSafetyTest | 90.18 | 99.50 | 88.24 | 89.40 |
| WildGuardTest | 89.17 | 68.47 | 82.75 | 88.90 |
| OpenAIMod | 72.00 | 79.69 | 76.78 | 72.10 |
| SafeRLHF | 70.04 | 44.36 | — | 64.46 |
| BeaverTails | 87.60 | 67.84 | — | 84.05 |
| **Macro Avg** | **~84.4** | **~79.9** | **~80.6** | **~84.0** |

### Headline Claims
- GuardReasoner 8B **surpasses GPT-4o+CoT by 5.74%** F1 on average
- GuardReasoner 8B **surpasses LLaMA Guard 3 8B by 20.84%** F1 on average
- GuardReasoner 1B achieves **88.51%** weighted average F1 on refusal detection, competitive with much larger models
- Even the **1B model** outperforms LLaMA Guard 3 8B on most benchmarks

### Explainability Evaluation
- Human annotators (3 STEM master's degree holders) re-labeled model errors using GuardReasoner's explanations
- **Agreement rate: 93.8%** — humans largely agree with the model's reasoning
- After correction using explanations, performance improved significantly across all tasks (+4–6 F1 points)

### Inference Efficiency (Critical Weakness)

| Metric | GuardReasoner 8B | CoLaGuard 8B (baseline for comparison) |
|--------|------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Latency (ms/query) | **4,407.8** | 342.0 |
| Tokens/query | **289.4** | 12.9 |
| EA-F1 (efficiency-adjusted) | **0.1838** | 2.3601 |

GuardReasoner's explicit CoT generation comes at a **~13× latency penalty** and **~22× token cost** compared to latent-reasoning alternatives like CoLaGuard.

## 5. Prior Results & Published Evaluations

### Original Paper Results (Jan 2025)
- Evaluated across **13 benchmarks** for 3 guardrail tasks
- Released models at 1B, 3B, and 8B scales
- Demonstrated strong cross-category generalization (open-ended harmful category detection)

### Third-Party Evaluations

#### SoK: Evaluating Jailbreak Guardrails for LLMs (2025)
- GuardReasoner (Pre) achieved the **lowest Attack Success Rate (ASR = 0.135)** among tested guardrails on Llama-3
- GuardReasoner (Post) achieved the **best Precision-Gated Recall (PGR = 0.141)**
- **However:** GuardReasoner showed significant vulnerability to **multi-turn attacks** (X-Teaming), with ASR often exceeding 90% in session-level evaluations
- **Latency criticism:** "GuardReasoner incurs substantial latency due to its reasoning overhead"
- **Memory criticism:** "GuardReasoner exhibits the highest memory footprint" among tested guardrails

#### Benchmarking Open-Source Safety Guard Models (Apr 2026)
- Evaluated 14 guard models on recall, precision, F1, accuracy, ROC-AUC, MCC
- GuardReasoner 3B ranked mid-tier on recall; Qwen Guard (83.97% recall) outperformed it
- Key finding: "Conservative models are dangerous" — ShieldGemma missed 54.51% of unsafe content despite high precision

#### GuardReasoner-VL (NeurIPS 2025)
- Extended the reasoning paradigm to vision-language models
- GuardReasoner-VL 7B vs. LLaMA Guard 4 12B: competitive or better on most benchmarks
- SPA-VL: 63.44% vs. 42.73% for LLaMA Guard 4

#### GuardReasoner-Omni (2026)
- First unified guardrail for text, image, video, and audio
- GuardReasoner-Omni 7B achieves **86.39%** average on prompt detection across all modalities
- Video: 95.86% on XD-Violence (comparable to specialized VAD models)
- Audio: 86.00% on prompt harmfulness, outperforming Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct (83.59%)

## 6. Limitations & Gaps

### Acknowledged by Authors

1. **Explainability evaluation is limited in scope**
   - Current evaluation only tests whether humans agree with predictions when shown CoT
   - Authors note this is a "crude proxy for explainability" that doesn't capture true model transparency
   - No evaluation of whether CoT *causes* the prediction (alignment between reasoning and verdict)

2. **Inference efficiency**
   - Generating explicit reasoning tokens adds ~150% latency vs. non-reasoning baselines
   - The 8B model takes **4.4 seconds per query** on an H100 GPU
   - The authors state future work will focus on "minimizing unnecessary reasoning steps" via model merging and lightweight reasoning

3. **Dependence on synthetic data quality**
   - Reasoning traces are synthesized by GPT-4o/Qwen3.5; hallucinations in teacher reasoning could propagate
   - No thorough analysis of reasoning trace accuracy vs. human expert reasoning

4. **Resource constraints**
   - A thorough explainability study requires significant human effort, which was constrained

### Identified by Third-Party Research

1. **Vulnerability to multi-turn attacks**
   - SoK evaluation found GuardReasoner (Post) highly vulnerable to X-Teaming adaptive multi-turn attacks
   - ASR exceeded 90% against session-level attacks — a critical gap for production deployment

2. **High latency makes pre-processing impractical**
   - SoK found GuardReasoner (Pre) was among the slowest pre-processing guardrails
   - For high-throughput applications, 4.4s/query is prohibitive

3. **High GPU memory footprint**
   - Largest memory usage among tested guardrails per SoK evaluation

4. **Limited cross-attack generalization**
   - LLM-based guardrails showed limited efficacy in detecting prompt injection attacks
   - PGRs ranged from 0.512 to 0.911 across guardrails on prompt injection

5. **Not universally superior across all attack types**
   - No single guardrail dominates; GuardReasoner excels at some attacks but fails at others

### What It Doesn't Cover

- **Real-time streaming moderation** — latency is too high for streaming applications
- **Policy-adaptive guardrailing** — GuardReasoner uses fixed safety categories; it doesn't dynamically adapt to changing policies (unlike newer work like PolicyShiftGuard, SingGuard)
- **Multilingual safety** — Primarily evaluated on English; limited coverage of low-resource languages
- **Adversarial robustness guarantees** — No formal/certified robustness claims
- **Long-context safety** — Evaluated on 2048-token contexts; no analysis of very long documents
- **Agentic/multi-step safety** — Does not evaluate safety in agentic loops or tool-use scenarios

## 7. Comparison with Other Safety Benchmarks & Guard Models

### GuardReasoner vs. Classification-Based Guardrails

| Dimension | GuardReasoner | LLaMA Guard 3 | ShieldGemma | WildGuard |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Paradigm** | Reasoning (CoT) | Classification | Classification | Classification + structure |
| **Explainability** | Explicit reasoning traces | Binary label only | Binary label only | Structured output |
| **OOD generalization** | Strong (reasoning transfers) | Weak (pattern-matching) | Weak | Moderate |
| **Latency** | Very high (~4.4s) | Low | Very low | Low |
| **Model size** | 1B/3B/8B | 8B | 2B/9B | 7B |
| **F1 (macro avg)** | ~84.4% (8B) | ~79.9% | ~68.8% (9B) | ~84.0% |
| **Open categories** | Yes | No (fixed taxonomy) | No (4 categories) | Yes (13 categories) |

### GuardReasoner vs. Reasoning Alternatives

| Dimension | GuardReasoner | GPT-4o+CoT | CoLaGuard | Qwen3Guard |
|-----------|--------------|------------|-----------|------------|
| **Cost** | Open-source, local | API-based ($$$) | Open-source | Open-source |
| **Reasoning type** | Explicit CoT tokens | Explicit CoT tokens | Latent reasoning (hidden) | Classification |
| **Latency** | ~4.4s | Variable | ~342ms | Low |
| **Performance** | Strong | Strong | Comparable | Best recall |
| **Transparency** | High (readable CoT) | High | Low (latent) | Low |

### GuardReasoner vs. Safety Benchmarks (HELM, BBH, HarmBench, etc.)

GuardReasoner is **not a benchmark** — it is a **guard model** that is *evaluated on* benchmarks. This is a critical distinction:

| Entity | Type | Purpose |
|--------|------|---------|
| **GuardReasoner** | Guard model (defense) | Moderate LLM/VLM inputs and outputs |
| **HarmBench** | Benchmark | Test harmful behavior elicitation |
| **HELM** | Holistic benchmark | Broad capability evaluation |
| **BBH** | Reasoning benchmark | Test reasoning capabilities |
| **Red Teaming datasets** | Attack collections | Jailbreak prompts and strategies |
| **SoK: Evaluating Jailbreak Guardrails** | Meta-evaluation | Systematic comparison of defenses |

GuardReasoner is **evaluated on** HarmBench, BeaverTails, ToxicChat, etc. — it does not define new evaluation tasks but rather demonstrates strong performance on existing ones. Its contribution is methodological (reasoning-based guardrailing) rather than definitional (new benchmarks).

### Position in the Safety Ecosystem

```
Attack Side                          Defense Side
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Jailbreak prompts  ────────┐
Adversarial examples ──────┼───▶  GuardReasoner (reasoning guard)
Multi-turn attacks ────────┤      LLaMA Guard (classification)
Prompt injection ──────────┤      WildGuard (structured)
Multimodal attacks ────────┘      ShieldGemma (lightweight)
                                  CoLaGuard (latent reasoning)
                                  GPT-4o Moderation (API)
```

GuardReasoner sits at the **high-performance, high-latency, high-explainability** corner of the design space. It trades efficiency for transparency and generalization — a trade-off that may be acceptable for high-stakes, low-throughput applications but problematic for real-time systems.

## 8. Key Takeaways for Evaluation Planning

1. **GuardReasoner is a guard model, not a benchmark.** If building an evaluation plan, you'd evaluate *against* GuardReasoner or *compare your guard model to* GuardReasoner using existing benchmarks like HarmBench, WildGuardTest, etc.

2. **The reasoning paradigm is its defining feature.** Any evaluation of GuardReasoner must assess both (a) final accuracy and (b) reasoning quality / alignment between CoT and verdict.

3. **Latency is a major deployment concern.** At 4.4s/query, it's unsuitable for real-time applications without optimization (quantization, speculative decoding, distillation).

4. **Multi-turn vulnerability is a critical gap.** The SoK evaluation found it fails against adaptive multi-turn attacks — this should be a focus area in any red-teaming evaluation.

5. **The model family is expanding rapidly.** From text → VL → omni (text/image/video/audio). The omni variant is the most current and comprehensive.

6. **Open-source availability makes it reproducible.** All models, data, and code are public — evaluation can be run locally without API dependencies.

7. **The field is moving toward efficiency.** Competitors like CoLaGuard achieve comparable F1 with 13× lower latency, suggesting the explicit-CoT approach may be supplanted by latent-reasoning alternatives in production.
